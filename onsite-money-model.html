Three things that decide this

These came out of the costing, not the plan. Each one moves the answer more than the growth rate does.

32%

Never charge the $1 on its own

Stripe AU is 1.7% + $0.30. On a single dollar that's 32 cents gone. Bank the matches and bill them once a month with the subscription — one invoice, one fee. The model assumes you do.

0.35%

$1 is far below what a match is worth

A filled 8-hour shift at the Award floor is $284.40 of wages. A labour-hire agency takes $43–71 of that. You're asking for one dollar. At $2 the Base case stops losing money.

12.7%

SMS quietly eats the match fee

Twilio to Australia is $0.0695 a message and your matcher wakes three phones per spot. On SMS alone that's 12.7% of all revenue. Web push first, SMS only for the booking, takes it to 4.4%.

The $33 and the $1 are not all yours

Both are GST-inclusive prices — that's almost certainly why you picked $33. Once you pass $75,000 turnover you must register for GST, and from then on you keep $30.00 of the subscription and $0.909 of the match. Every figure on this page is already net of that.

What I'd change

Price the match at what it saves, not at what feels small

$1 is a rounding error on a $284 shift, and it prices the hardest part of your product — finding a stranger who turns up at 6am — below the cost of the text messages that find him. $2–3 is still a twentieth of an agency margin and no subbie will blink. Move the slider and watch Year 3 swing.

The subscription is the business; matching is the hook

  • Matching revenue leaks by design. A boss meets a good worker once, then rings him direct. You can't stop that, so don't build on it.
  • The records can't leak. Hours, approvals, the pay run, the worker pack — he opens those every week whether or not he's hiring strangers. That's what the $33 buys, and why the take-up rate matters more than any growth number here.
  • So sell the boss app first. Get it into 20 subbies in one suburb; their crews become your worker supply for free. Liquidity from the single-player tool, not from ads.

Two costs to design out before you launch

Switch notifications to web push — the app is already a PWA, so it's free and it saves more than the match fee earns. And bill one invoice per boss per month, never per match. Both are build decisions, not pricing ones, and both are cheaper to make now than later.

Costed against: Stripe AU domestic card 1.7% + $0.30, falling to 1.65% + $0.30 on 1 Oct 2026 · Twilio SMS to Australia $0.0695 ex-GST · GST 10%, registration required above $75k turnover · Award casual floor $35.55/h (MA000020 CW1).

Fixed costs assumed: hosting $45–950/mo on a step as you grow, software $150, insurance + accounting + legal $400, support staff $6,000 loaded per head. Support time 6 minutes per boss per month.
